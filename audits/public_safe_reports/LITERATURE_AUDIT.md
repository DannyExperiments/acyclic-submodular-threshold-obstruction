# Q335 literature and source check

Search completed 27 November 2026. High confidence on the cited question, hypotheses and verified overlap. A bounded exact-phrase and forward search located no published presentation of the six-ground-element witness. Priority remains open.

## Exact target and closest earlier work

Elbracht asks whether a finite lattice can contain a family \(P\) closed under at least one corner of every pair, with an acyclic full dependency digraph and without a submodular strict-threshold representation. His dissertation Question 5.1.6 is printed p. 199, following the dependency definitions on pp. 195–196. The catalog gives the three arc types explicitly. A real-valued strict-threshold representation can be translated to make both the function and threshold nonnegative on a finite lattice. The witness actually excludes real-valued functions without any sign restriction. [Elbracht dissertation](https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/spag/dm/papers/Dissertation_Christian_Elbracht.pdf#page=199), [pinned Q335](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/combinatorics-graph-theory-part-1.md#q335).

Elbracht, Kneip and Teegen, *The Structure of Submodular Separation Systems*, §4, gives the identical question as Question 4.5. Lemma 4.1 gives strict decrease along every dependency arc and Corollary 4.2 deduces acyclicity from representability. Theorem 4.3 exhibits structural submodularity without a threshold in a distributive universe, using a directed cycle. Figure 2 isolates a cycle entirely outside the partial lattice while the inner digraph is acyclic. The six-element witness makes both parts acyclic. The authors' equivalence with Question 4.4 uses an involutive doubling construction, so that equivalent consequence needs no separate novelty claim. Teegen's dissertation repeats the two formulations as Questions 8.6–8.7, printed p. 177. [Author text, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.13162v2#S4), [Teegen dissertation](https://d-nb.info/1243470445/34).

## Exact overlap with a published Boolean example

Simmons, Bansal and Cheriyan, *Symmetric Submodular Functions, Uncrossable Functions, and Structural Submodularity*, Algorithm 1 and Example 2, construct a fifteen-member family \(F\) on \(\{0,\ldots,7\}\). Their condition tests crossing pairs and also asks for a difference corner. In their displayed family the disjoint members \(\{3\},\{4\}\) have neither ordinary corner in \(F\). Their Proposition 8 forbids symmetric submodular thresholds using difference inequalities, and their paper does not address the dependency digraph of the Elbracht–Kneip–Teegen question. Adding only the empty set yields an acyclic intersection-closed family that still admits a general submodular threshold, as the accompanying integer table verifies. [Example 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00140v1#S3), [Proposition 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00140v1#S5).

The same source does yield a second exact Q335 witness by a short adaptation and a finite check. Put \(U=\{1,\ldots,7\}\), dropping the unused element \(0\) from the published example, and take \(P'=F\cup\{U\setminus A:A\in F\}\cup\{\varnothing,U\}\). The replay checks all corners of its 32 members and all 3,494 dependency arcs on \(2^U\). The arcs sort into fourteen layers. Retaining element \(0\) and taking complements in the original universe also yields a witness, with 8,402 arcs and sixteen layers.

Write \(V_1=\{1,3,5,7\}\), \(V_2=\{2,3,6,7\}\), \(V_3=\{4,5,6,7\}\). The \(k=3\) sum in Proposition 8 applies the difference inequality of symmetric submodular functions to \((V_1,V_2)\), \((\{1,5\},V_3)\) and \((\{2,6\},\{4,6,7\})\). It yields
\[
g(\{1\})+g(\{2\})+g(\{4,7\})\leq g(V_1)+g(V_2)+g(V_3).
\]
The left sets are absent from \(P'\): they are absent from \(F\), their complements in the seven-element ground set have at least five elements, every set in \(F\) has at most four, and none of the three is an extremum. The right sets lie in \(F\). Because \(P'\) is complement-closed, the symmetrization \(g(A)=f(A)+f(U\setminus A)\) of any hypothetical submodular threshold function \(f\) induces \(P'\) at twice its threshold. The inequality thus gives a contradiction. The seven-element adaptation is a deduction from the published family and inequality together with our closure and acyclicity checks. The release's main witness is a distinct nine-member family on six ground elements, using union and intersection inequalities directly.

## Other nearby results and limits

- *The Unravelling Problem* by Elbracht, Kneip and Teegen addresses iterated removal of one member. Its Theorem 2 gives removal for order-induced families, and §5 gives a woven family in a nondistributive lattice from which no member can be removed. In that counterexample every member therefore has an incoming inner dependency arc, giving an inner cycle. Its obstruction differs from Q335. The original question concerning deletion for Boolean families is separate. [Author text](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.12565v1#S5).
- The appendix's finite criterion is standard linear inequality duality applied to submodularity and a normalized separation margin. Farkas' lemma, conic Carathéodory and the elementary diminishing-returns square inequalities are prior tools. The result here is an exact finite obstruction certificate for the given witness. [Farkas](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1902.124.1), [Rockafellar, Convex Analysis, §17](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691015866/convex-analysis), [Fujishige, Submodular Functions and Optimization](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~fujishig/book-NH.html).

Searches covered the dissertations and author articles, exact question numbers and phrases, cited and citing results located by author/title and arXiv identifiers, and terms for structurally submodular set families, uncrossability, pliability, threshold representability and dependency graphs. This leaves unindexed work and variants of the prior construction open. No minimality claim is made for the number of members, ground elements or ambient lattice elements.
